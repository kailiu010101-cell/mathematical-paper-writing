# Sources and evidence

This writing profile was developed from two first-author papers by Kai Liu of Tianjin University, with two coauthored papers used as supplementary evidence. Page numbers below refer to one-based PDF pages. Bibliographic facts were checked against the source PDFs.

The three-use guideline for auxiliary notation, indexed constants, English manuscript language, and proofreading boundaries are explicit writing preferences. They are not claimed to be statistical findings about the author's complete body of work. Reading focused on mathematical exposition, not independent verification of every mathematical result.

## How to use or extend the evidence

1. Prefer patterns repeated in A and B. Treat C and D as supplementary, and label one-paper variations as such.
2. The writing profile can be used without reopening the papers. Consult full text only when a specific attribution or equation needs verification.
3. When adding sources, verify author identity, title, version, author order, and affiliation. Do not merge authors solely because their names match.
4. Record which parts were actually read and the relevant page or section locations. Do not infer proof organization from an abstract.
5. Distinguish source observations, explicit writing preferences, and general application guidance. Do not treat citation style or journal formatting as an author's unique language preference.
